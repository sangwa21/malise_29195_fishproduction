## Source 1: Official Government Report

**Title:** Annual Report 2024/2025

**Organization:** Ministry of Agriculture and Animal Resources (MINAGRI), Rwanda.

**Year:** 2025

**Summary:** The report provides information about Rwanda's agricultural sector, including fish production. It reports total fish production of approximately 52,439.3 metric tonnes during the 2024/2025 financial year.

**Relevance:** This report provides official Rwanda fish production statistics that can help establish the background and objectives of the project.

**Source link:** [MINAGRI Annual Report 2024/2025](https://www.minagri.gov.rw/)

## Source 2: Research Paper

**Title:** Aquaculture and aquafeed in Rwanda: Current status and perspectives

**Authors:** Niyibizi, L., Vidakovic, A., Norman Haldén, A., Rukera Tabaro, S., & Lundh, T.

**Year:** 2023

**Journal:** Journal of Applied Aquaculture, 35(3), 743–764.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10454438.2021.2024315

**Summary:** This study examined 67 fish farms in Rwanda. It identified three commonly farmed fish species: Nile tilapia, common carp, and North African catfish. It also examined fish farming methods and local feed ingredients.

**Relevance:** This research helps identify fish species and farming methods that can be considered when designing the project's database.
## Source 3: Existing Fisheries Monitoring System

**System name:** PeskAAS (Peskas)

**Organization:** WorldFish and research partners

**Year:** 2020

**Description:** PeskAAS is an open-source digital fisheries monitoring and analytics system developed and tested in Timor-Leste. It collects, processes, analyzes, and displays fishing catch information through an interactive dashboard.

**APA Reference:**

Tilley, A., Dos Reis Lopes, J., & Wilkinson, S. P. (2020). PeskAAS: A near-real-time, open-source monitoring and analytics system for small-scale fisheries. PLoS ONE, 15(11), e0234760. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234760

**Relevance:** This system demonstrates how fisheries information can be organized and presented through dashboards. It provides ideas for the interactive dashboard planned for this project.

**Source link:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0234760 
## Source 4: Dataset

**Dataset name:** FAO Fishery and Aquaculture Statistics (FishStat)

**Organization:** Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO)

**Year:** 2026 (latest release)

**Description:** FAO FishStat provides annual global statistics on aquaculture and capture fisheries production. The data includes production quantities by country, species, and year.

**APA Reference:**

Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations. (2026). Global fisheries and aquaculture production statistics (FishStat). https://www.fao.org/statistics/data-collection/fishery-and-aquaculture/en

**Relevance:** This dataset will provide the production records for the project. The system will use relevant Rwanda records to analyze fish production trends, compare aquaculture and capture fisheries, and generate dashboard summaries.

**Data license:** Check the specific dataset's licence and attribution requirements before redistribution. 
## Source 5: Research Paper

**Title:** Current status of Cyprinids abundance and diversity and their potential for aquaculture production in Rwanda

**Authors:** Mwimba, R., Rukera Tabaro, S., & Syaghalirwa, N. M. M.

**Year:** 2024

**Journal:** Rwanda Journal of Agricultural Sciences.

**Description:** This research investigated the abundance and diversity of Cyprinid fish species in different water bodies in Rwanda. It identified eight species and discussed their potential for aquaculture production.

**Relevance:** This study helps identify fish species found in Rwanda and supports the importance of monitoring species diversity and sustainable fish production.

**Source link:** [African Journals Online](https://www.ajol.info/)
